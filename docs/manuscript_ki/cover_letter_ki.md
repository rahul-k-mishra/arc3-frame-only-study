To the Editors of KI - Künstliche Intelligenz

Dear Editors,

I submit the manuscript "A Frame-Only Census of Game Mechanics Built on ARC-AGI-3 Did Not Transfer to MiniGrid/BabyAI or Procgen in a Preregistered Test" as a Technical Contribution.

Before any confirmatory run, I registered the claim that a fixed, frame-only description of a game's mechanics, five assumptions read from the screen by a battery of probes whose parameters were frozen on eight ARC-AGI-3 games, would describe unseen games from other benchmarks as their source code does, and would predict on which games agents that rely on those assumptions complete the first level. I ran the instrument and three agents on 161 MiniGrid/BabyAI environments under two action conditions and on 14 Procgen games, 10,500 runs in all, and scored the instrument against truth tables read from each game's source code. The registered validity test failed on both benchmarks, the registered prediction test passed for no agent, and by the registered outcome table the result is a refutation. A baseline built only from the size and variety of the environments ranked them better than the instrument on all six primary MiniGrid/BabyAI lines. The article reports these results exactly as the frozen analysis code wrote them, lists every departure from the protocol, and keeps an exploratory diagnosis, written after the outcome, apart from the registered results. I believe the question it answers, whether a cheap description of game mechanics built on one benchmark carries over to others, concerns anyone who evaluates agents on unseen games.

An earlier version of the article is a preprint on Zenodo under CC-BY 4.0: https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23375065. The supplementary material, submitted as Online Resource 1, is also on Zenodo under CC-BY 4.0: https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23614519. All data and code are public in the repository https://github.com/rahul-k-mishra/arc3-frame-only-study under the MIT license, and the protocol is registered on the Open Science Framework at https://osf.io/2znhw (v1) and https://osf.io/zu5vq (v1.1).

The registered protocol fixed the consequence of a refutation as "repository record only, at most an arXiv note". I chose to seek peer review of the negative result instead, and the article declares this as a deviation from the registered protocol. It changes no analysis and no number.
