Dear Editors,

I submit the manuscript "A Frame-Only Census of Game Mechanics Built on ARC-AGI-3 Failed a Preregistered Generality Test on MiniGrid/BabyAI and Procgen" for consideration as a research article in the Journal of Artificial General Intelligence.

The article reports a preregistered test of generality. A fixed, frame-only instrument for describing the mechanics of unseen games was built and frozen on one benchmark (ARC-AGI-3) and then tested, under a protocol registered before any confirmatory run, on games from two other benchmarks (MiniGrid/BabyAI and Procgen). The registered answer is no: the instrument failed the registered test of whether it reports the games' mechanics as their source code states them, and its score passed the registered test of predicting where agents complete the first level for no agent. The article sets out what that implies for transferring instruments between benchmarks and for registered claims in the evaluation of general agents.

An earlier version of this article is a preprint on Zenodo under CC-BY 4.0: https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23375065.

The supplement to this version is on the same Zenodo record as version 2: https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23614519

The registered protocol's venue clause for this outcome read "repository record only, at most an arXiv note". I chose to seek peer review of the negative result instead. This departure is declared in the manuscript (Section 3.10) and changes no analysis and no number.

Compared with the Zenodo preprint, this version is rewritten for a specialist readership in artificial general intelligence and shortened: the full tables, the amendment trail, the exclusion lists, the provenance of every number and the hashes of the frozen files are moved to a supplement. It is not under review elsewhere.
